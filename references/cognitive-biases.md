# Cognitive Biases & Vietlott Prediction-Method Myths

## Foundation

- Tversky, A., & Kahneman, D. (1971). *Belief in the Law of Small Numbers*. Psychological Bulletin.
- Tversky, A., & Kahneman, D. (1972). *Subjective Probability: A Judgment of Representativeness*. Cognitive Psychology.
- Langer, E., & Roth, J. (1975). *Heads I Win, Tails It's Chance*. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology.
- Wagenaar, W. (1988). *Paradoxes of Gambling Behaviour*. Erlbaum.
- Griffiths, M. (1994). *The Role of Cognitive Bias and Skill in Fruit Machine Gambling*. British Journal of Psychology.
- Clotfelter, C., & Cook, C. (1993). *The 'Gambler's Fallacy' in Lottery Play*. Management Science.
- Croson, R., & Sundali, J. (2005). *The Gambler's Fallacy and the Hot Hand*. Journal of Risk and Uncertainty.

This reference explains the psychological mechanisms behind common Vietlott "phương pháp dự đoán" (prediction method) myths and how to address them with evidence.

## Core Fallacies (Vietnamese-context)

### 1. Gambler's fallacy (Ngụy biện người cờ bạc)

**Fallacy:** past random events influence future ones. "So 7 ra 5 lan roi, lan sau chac khong ra nua" (7 came out 5 times, so next time it won't).

**Reality:** in independent random events, past outcomes do not influence future ones. Each Vietlott draw is a fresh start.

**Research:** Tversky & Kahneman (1971); Clotfelter & Cook (1993) found real lottery players bet *less* on recently winning numbers; Croson & Sundali (2005) documented the fallacy in rapid-draw casino betting — directly applicable to Keno.

**How to explain:**
```
May quay so Vietlott khong co bo nho. Moi ky quay la doc lap hoan toan.
Xac suat cua bat ky so nao cung nhu nhau moi lan quay.

Vi du tung dong xuat: neu ra nguoi 5 lan lien tiep,
xac suat nguoi o lan thu 6 van la 50%. Dong xuat khong "biet" no vua ra nguoi.
Vietlott cung vay: moi ky quay la mot su kien ngau nhin moi.
```

**Teaching strategy:** (1) acknowledge the intuition ("trong long cam thay phai can bang"); (2) explain independence; (3) coin/dice analogy; (4) "evening out" happens over thousands of draws, not the next draw.

### 2. Hot and cold numbers (So hot / so lanh / soi cầu)

**Fallacy:** numbers that appear frequently are "hot" (should keep appearing) or haven't appeared in a while are "cold" / "due" (sắp ra). "Soi cầu" sites publish hot/cold tables.

**Reality:** in a fair lottery, every number has exactly equal probability every draw. Apparent patterns are random clustering.

**Research:** Clotfelter & Cook (1993); empirical studies of draw histories show **no predictive power** in hot/cold analysis; tested against random selection, hot/cold strategies show no advantage.

**How to explain:**
```
So hot/cold khong ton tai — do la cum ngau nhien.
Tung dong xuat 10 lan, ban co the duoc: N N N X X N X N N N (8 nguoi, 2 sap).
Nghia la nguoi "dang hot" khong? Khong — chi la bien thien ngau nhien.
Sau 1000 lan thi se gan 50/50. So Vietlott cung vay: mot so xuat hien nhieu
trong cac ky gan day chi la nhieu ngau nhien, khong du bao ky sau.
May quay khong theo doi so nao "den luot".
```

**Empirical evidence:** studies of lottery histories find no predictive power; any patterns are random variation. This has been extensively studied.

### 3. Representativeness heuristic (Ngụy biện đại diện)

**Fallacy:** random sequences should "look random" (no patterns, near-even distribution).

**Reality:** true random sequences contain clusters, streaks, and apparent patterns. The combination 1-2-3-4-5-6 has exactly the same probability as any other specific combination: 1 in 8,145,060 (Mega).

**Research:** Tversky & Kahneman (1972) — people reject truly random sequences as "not random enough."

### 4. Illusion of control (Ao tuong kiem soat)

**Fallacy:** one can influence/predict random outcomes through skill, knowledge, or rituals. Manifestations in Vietlott:
- Choosing "so dep" (lucky numbers): ngay sinh, ngay chung (birthdays, anniversaries) — biases selection toward 1-31.
- Using "phương pháp dự đoán" / "cong thuc" / soi-cau software.
- Believing certain agents/points of sale or times are "may mắn."
- Avoiding recently winning numbers.
- Playing the same numbers every draw "cho den khi trung."

**Reality:** lottery draws are random and cannot be influenced by player actions. Birthday-bias means many players share 1-31, so if those win, the pari-mutuel jackpot is split more ways (a real EV cost of "lucky number" selection).

**Research:** Langer & Roth (1975); Griffiths (1994).

**How to address:**
```
Toi hieu ban cam thay lua chon cua ban co y nghia, nhung voi may quay ngau nhien
thi khong. Bong Vietlott khong "biet" ban chon so sinh nhat. Moi to hop co
xac suat giong nhau.

Them nua: chon so sinh nhat (1-31) nghia la nhieu nguoi cung chon — neu trung,
jackpot chia nhieu nguoi hon, gia tri ky vong con giam xuong.
```

### 5. Near-miss effect (Hiệu suýt trúng)

**Fallacy:** almost winning ("thua 1 so thoi!") signals skill or rising probability.

**Reality:** near-misses are statistically common and do not predict future success.

**Research:** Griffiths (1994) — near-misses activate the same brain regions as wins; people read "almost" as "close" rather than "missed by far."

**How to explain:**
```
Mega 6/45: trung 5 so co ti le 1/34,808; jackpot 1/8,145,860.
Trung 5 la 234 lan de hon trung 6. "Suýt trúng" la pho bien theo xac suat.
Trung 5 khong co nghia ban "gần" trung 6 — ban hoan toan sai hoac hoan toan dung.
```

### 6. Availability heuristic (Ngụy biện kha dụng)

**Fallacy:** overestimating likelihood of memorable/widely-reported events (jackpot winners on VTV/news).

**Reality:** jackpot winners are extremely rare; media coverage makes them seem common.

**Research:** Tversky & Kahneman (1973). For Mega jackpot, 8,145,059 losing tickets per winner (~99.99999% lose).

**How to explain:**
```
Bao chi chi dang nguoi trung jackpot, khong bao nhieu trieu nguoi khong trung.
Nen chung ta co an tuong sai rang trung la pho bien. Thu te: ~8 trieu ve thua
moi 1 ve trung jackpot Mega.
```

### 7. Rapid-draw fallacy (Keno-specific)

**Fallacy:** "Keno quay nhanh nen de thang / de bat chuoi" — rapid draws make patterns "visible."

**Reality:** rapid draws *intensify* pattern-belief but each draw is independent. Croson & Sundali (2005) document stronger gambler's-fallacy and hot-hand beliefs in rapid-draw games. The danger of Keno is frequency (144 draws/day), not per-draw difficulty.

## Debunking Strategies (Vietnamese-context)

### Strategy 1: Acknowledge then redirect
```
User: "So 7 dang hot, ra hoai!"
You: "Toi hieu vi sao ban nghi vay — 7 xuat hien nhieu hon gan day. Nhung voi
     may quay ngau nhien, moi so co xac suat giong nhau moi ky. May khong theo
     doi so nao 'hot'. Ban muon toi giai thich toan hoc cho dieu nay khong?"
```

### Strategy 2: Concrete examples
```
User: "So cua toi 'den luot' roi, chua trung bao gio!"
You: "Nghi nhu the nay: tung dong xuat 10 lan nguoi lien tiep, lan 11 co phai sap
     khong? Khong — van 50/50. Dong xuat khong nho cac lan truoc. So Vietlott
     cung vay, moi ky doc lap."
```

### Strategy 3: Empirical evidence
```
User: "Nhung toi theo doi so hot va no chay!"
You: "Nha nghien cuu da test chien luoc hot/cold voi chon ngau nhien qua hang
     ngan ky quay. Khong co loi the nao. Bat ky mau nao ban thay deu la bien
     thien ngau nhien, khong du bao ky sau. Dieu nay da duoc nghien cuu ky."
```

### Strategy 4: Shift to real mathematics
```
User: "Du doan cap so dep cho toi!"
You: "Toi khong the du doan so, va day la vi sao: ky quay Vietlott duoc thiet
     ke de khong the du doan toan hoc. Nhung TOI co the giup ban hieu:
     - Xac suat thuc su cua cac tro choi Vietlott
     - Cach xac suat hoat dong trong ky quay
     - Gia tri ky vong cua moi ve
     - Cach nghi ve chi tieu Vietlott nhu giai tri
     Ban muon toi giai thich dieu nao?"
```

## Teaching Framework

1. **Validate the intuition** — acknowledge the fallacy feels right; our brains are wired for patterns; this is a common human error.
2. **Explain the reality** — clear language; coin/dice analogies; show independence.
3. **Provide evidence** — cite research; note it has been extensively studied; experts agree.
4. **Redirect to productive thinking** — offer real probability, EV, responsible spending; focus on what is controllable (budgeting).

## Research summaries

- **Tversky & Kahneman (1971):** people expect small samples to reflect the population -> hot/cold fallacies.
- **Langer & Roth (1975):** familiarity/involvement breeds confidence in chance tasks -> elaborate "systems" that don't work.
- **Griffiths (1994):** systematic cognitive distortions (illusion of control, interpretive bias, predictive bias) in gambling.
- **Croson & Sundali (2005):** rapid-draw games intensify fallacious pattern-belief -> Keno relevance.
- **Clotfelter & Cook (1993):** real lottery players avoid recently winning numbers -> empirical gambler's fallacy.
