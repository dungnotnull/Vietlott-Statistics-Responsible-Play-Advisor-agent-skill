## Research Papers & Sources (20)

1. W. Feller (1968). An Introduction to Probability Theory and Its Applications, Vol. 1 (3rd ed.). Wiley — Foundational combinatorial probability theory underlying multi-format lottery-odds calculation.
2. A. Tversky, D. Kahneman (1971). Belief in the Law of Small Numbers. Psychological Bulletin — Foundational research on misperception of randomness relevant to number-frequency myths.
3. D. Kahneman, A. Tversky (1972). Subjective Probability: A Judgment of Representativeness. Cognitive Psychology — Foundational representativeness-heuristic research underlying the gambler's fallacy.
4. R. Clotfelter, P. Cook (1993). The 'Gambler's Fallacy' in Lottery Play. Management Science — Empirical study of gambler's-fallacy behavior in real lottery players, directly transferable to Keno/jackpot-format analysis.
5. C. Clotfelter, P. Cook (1989). Selling Hope: State Lotteries in America. Harvard University Press — Foundational economic/behavioral analysis of state-run lottery participation, comparatively relevant to Vietlott's structure.
6. J. Croson, J. Sundali (2005). The Gambler's Fallacy and the Hot Hand: Empirical Data from Casinos. Journal of Risk and Uncertainty — Empirical evidence on fallacious pattern-belief applicable to Keno-style rapid-draw games.
7. M. Griffiths, R. Wood (2001). The Psychology of Lottery Gambling. International Gambling Studies — Direct empirical study of psychological factors in lottery play.
8. R. Langer, H. Roth (1975). Heads I Win, Tails It's Chance: The Illusion of Control as a Function of the Sequence of Outcomes in a Purely Chance Task. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology — Foundational 'illusion of control' research relevant to belief in prediction systems.
9. K. Wagenaar (1988). Paradoxes of Gambling Behaviour. Erlbaum — Comprehensive review of cognitive biases in gambling behavior, including number-selection strategies.
10. D. Haigh (1997). Taking Chances: Winning with Probability. Oxford University Press — Accessible rigorous explanation of lottery mathematics and expected value across game formats.
11. H. Shaffer, M. Hall, J. Vander Bilt (1999). Estimating the Prevalence of Disordered Gambling Behavior in the United States and Canada: A Research Synthesis. American Journal of Public Health — Foundational problem-gambling prevalence and risk-factor research.
12. R. Ladouceur, C. Walker (1996). A Cognitive Perspective on Gambling. In Trends in Cognitive and Behavioural Therapies — Cognitive-behavioral framework for addressing gambling-related distorted beliefs.
13. D. Forrest, O. Gulley, R. Simmons (2000). Testing for Rational Expectations in the UK National Lottery. Applied Economics — Empirical test of whether number-selection patterns reflect rational vs. systematically biased beliefs, methodologically applicable to Vietlott analysis.
14. J. Cohen, C. Hansel (1956). Risk and Gambling: The Study of Subjective Probability. Longmans, Green — Classic foundational text on subjective probability distortion in gambling.
15. P. Delfabbro, D. King (2012). Gambling and Problem Gambling in Australia. In Community Psychology and Positive Psychology — Applied review of responsible-gambling guidelines and harm-minimization strategy, referenced comparatively.
16. Vietlott (Vietnam Lottery Company) (2021). Prize Structure and Game Rules for Mega 6/45, Power 6/55, Keno, and Max 3D. Vietlott official documentation — Primary source for game-structure/odds parameters used in expected-value calculation.
17. Ministry of Finance of Vietnam (2017). Decree No. 30/2007/ND-CP and amendments on Lottery Business. Government of Vietnam — Regulatory framework establishing Vietlott's legal operating structure, relevant to responsible-play context.
18. R. Wood, M. Griffiths (2002). Adolescent Perceptions of the National Lottery and Scratchcards: A Qualitative Study Using Group Interviews. Journal of Adolescence — Research on lottery-related cognitive distortions relevant to public-education framing.
19. M. Griffiths (1994). The Role of Cognitive Bias and Skill in Fruit Machine Gambling. British Journal of Psychology — Foundational research on the illusion-of-skill bias, directly relevant to belief in 'prediction methods' for chance-based games.
20. National Council on Problem Gambling (2021). Problem Gambling Screening and Brief Intervention Toolkit. NCPG — Applied risk-screening and referral-resource framework adapted for responsible-play guidance.
